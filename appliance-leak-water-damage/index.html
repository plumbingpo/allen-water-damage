Appliance Leak Water Damage in Allen: What to Know

Three appliances cause most of the leaks we see. When the supply hose on a washing machine splits, water flows under full pressure until somebody turns it off. With a dishwasher the leak is typically slow, coming from the door seal or the drain hose where the cabinets hide it. The ice maker line on a refrigerator is a thin tube, and it can drip for months behind the unit. Cleanup is different for each of them.

For homeowners in Allen, we begin by sliding the machine out to see how far the water traveled. For a kitchen, the usual places are below the flooring and under the cabinets. In laundry rooms it means the wall behind the machine and the rooms next door, or the ceiling below if the laundry is upstairs. We extract what is standing, measure what has soaked in, and set drying equipment sized to the wet area.

Right Now

Before We Arrive

  1. Turn Off the ApplianceStop the cycle and unplug the machine if the outlet and floor around it are dry. Otherwise, use the breaker to cut its power.
  2. Shut Off the Water LineFor a washing machine, close both the cold and hot valves on the wall at its back. Look below the sink for the valve that feeds a dishwasher. The small valve for an ice maker line sits behind the refrigerator or underneath.
  3. Mop Up What You CanMop or towel up what you can reach so it stops spreading under cabinets and flooring. Do not vacuum unless the machine is rated for wet and dry use.
  4. Leave the Door ShutIf the washer or dishwasher has not drained, leave it shut. The entire load of water may spill across the floor if the door is opened mid-cycle.
  5. Photograph the Failed PartKeep the part that failed, whether a hose, a fitting or a valve, and get photos of it. It may be requested by the manufacturer of the appliance and by your insurance company.
Common Causes

How It Usually Starts

  • A dishwasher with a failed pump seal, a cracked drain hose or a worn door gasket.
  • Refrigerators that leak at floor level because the drain pan has cracked or the defrost drain is clogged.
  • A failed water inlet valve that keeps filling the washer or dishwasher after it should stop.
  • Rubber washing machine supply hoses that weaken with age and split under pressure.
  • A washer drain hose that slips out of the standpipe, or a clogged standpipe.
If It Is Left

What Happens if You Wait

Flooring and Subfloor

Water slides beneath laminate, vinyl plank and hardwood. Cupping and swelling show up in the boards, and the subfloor can stay damp long after the surface appears dry.

Rooms Next Door

Water may pour from a washer hose for hours on end. Hallways, bedrooms and closets get wet as it travels under the walls, and carpet and pad are soaked a good distance from the laundry.

Mold Behind Appliances

Hardly anyone looks at the dark, warm space behind a dishwasher or refrigerator. Mold on the floor and wall can be fed by a slow drip in that spot well before it is noticed.

Wall Bases and Trim

Drywall edges and baseboards pull up water that stands on a kitchen or laundry room floor. Often the worst of it is on the wall in back of the appliance, where it went unseen.

Coverage

Insurance and Appliance Leak Water Damage

Homeowners policies often cover sudden and accidental events, and a burst hose or a machine overflowing with no warning is that kind of event. Payment typically goes toward the damage the water did, and the appliance or the part that failed is not included. A claim is harder when an ice maker line or dishwasher seeped slowly, since coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks. Some insurers also ask about the age and upkeep of the hose. Check your policy, keep the failed part and take photos before cleanup.

Cost

What Drives the Price

  • Whether mold has started and has to be removed before the area can be dried.
  • Whether this was a sudden flood or slow seepage, and which machine it came from.
  • The time that passed before anyone found the leak, which matters most for slow drips at the back of a unit.
  • Whether cabinets and flooring can be dried in place or need to be removed.
